The problem

Automated screening is losing the people companies want to hire.

Most screening software matches resumes against fixed criteria. The research shows what that costs employers, and why candidates have stopped trusting it.

88%

of employers agree that qualified, high-skill candidates are screened out because they don't match the exact criteria in the job description.

Harvard Business School & Accenture, 2021

27M+

"hidden workers" in the US alone: people who want to work and have, or could build, the skills employers ask for.

Harvard Business School & Accenture, 2021

26%

of job applicants trust AI to evaluate them fairly, in a survey of 2,918 candidates.

Gartner, 2025

38%

of applicants want assurance that a human reviewed the AI's evaluation before a decision was made.

Gartner, 2025

The failure isn't AI itself, it's rigid criteria applied without context. A job description can't say which requirements truly matter, which can be learned, or what the team actually needs. SmartHire asks HR exactly that before a single resume is scored.

Our approach

From keyword gates to criteria HR actually defines.

The same resumes, judged two different ways.

Aspect Keyword filtering SmartHire
Where the criteria come from ✕The job description, taken literally ✓The job description plus HR's answers to 3–8 questions written for that specific role
How resumes are read ✕Exact titles and keywords must appear ✓Experience is read for meaning, so equivalent skills and unconventional paths count
What HR gets back ✕Pass or reject, with no explanation ✓A score, strengths with evidence, gaps, and a written justification for every candidate
Must-haves vs. nice-to-haves ✕Every listed requirement weighs the same ✓HR's answers decide what is a deal-breaker and what can be learned on the job
Gaming the system ✕Keyword stuffing raises the match rate ✓Instructions hidden in a resume are ignored and flagged as a concern
Who decides ✕The filter rejects before anyone looks ✓Every resume is ranked and kept; HR decides who moves forward
How it works

Three steps, about as long as a coffee break.

1

Add the role and resumes

Paste the job description and drop in resumes as PDFs or a single zip file.

Text is extracted from each PDF. Files that can't be read are flagged, not silently dropped.

Coming in v2 Import straight from a Google Form, cover letters included.

2

Answer what the JD leaves out

SmartHire writes 3–8 questions for this specific role: true must-haves, first-90-day goals, team setup, deal-breakers.

A clear JD gets fewer questions; a vague or senior one gets more.

3

Review a ranked shortlist

Every resume is scored against the JD and your answers, with the reasoning attached.

Download the results as a CSV or save them to your Google Sheet; HR can get an email summary.

For every candidate

A recommendation you can explain.

Nothing is a black box. Every part of the evaluation is written out, saved, and open to review.

Score out of 100

Weighted across technical skills, experience, fit with HR's context, and overall potential.

Strengths with evidence

Each strength points to something specific in the resume, not a generic label.

Gaps and concerns

What's missing or unclear, so interviews can probe the right things.

Written justification

A short paragraph explaining how the score was reached against your criteria.

Recommendation

Strong Yes, Yes, Maybe or No, plus an interview priority to plan your week.

No resumes stored

Resume text is used only during the screening session. Keep the results as a CSV, or save them to your own Google Sheet.
